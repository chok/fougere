A frond can live in another repository, with its own team and release cycle. Your app still calls
it. What has to reach your repository is its **contract** — its entities and operations — never its
code.

## Get the contract

Two commands, depending on how the other team shares it:

| | What it does | When |
|---|---|---|
| [`fougere sync`](/reference/cli#fougere-sync-consume-a-remote-frond) | asks the running frond for its card (`rpc.discover`) and writes its entities and facade into `.fougere/remotes/` | the frond is running — also the only way to use a frond written in another language |
| [`fougere build-frond`](/reference/cli#fougere-build-frond-publish-a-fronds-entities) | compiles its `entities/` into a package you install | the other team publishes its contract as a dependency |

Either way `@fronds/<name>` resolves, so your pages and handlers import the frond the same way as
one of your own.

## Call it

Give it its address, as for any frond in another process:

```ts
// fougere.config.ts
fronds: { billing: { remote: 'https://billing.internal' } },
```

The two processes must be able to reach each other, and the receiver must know who calls it:
[Deploy a split](/docs/run/deploy#splitting-in-production) and
[Secure calls between processes](/docs/run/secure).

## Facts across repositories

**Finding the listeners.** When the other frond has an address in your `fronds:`, its card says which
facts it accepts, and an announcement reaches it as a call — nothing more to write. A call keeps
nothing, though: a listener that is down misses the fact. When that matters, or when the other side
has no address you can call, put a **carrier** under the announcement — a queue or a broker of your
own. Both sides meet on the fact's name, read off their own code:

```ts
const app = await createApp({
  root: import.meta.dirname,
  createContainer,
  onEmit: (fact, payload) => publishToYourBroker(fact, payload),   // out
});

// in — the same local dispatch, validation and links included
await app.deliver(fact, payload);
// what to subscribe to, read off your own signatures
app.listensTo();                                                   // ['postPublished']
```

**Knowing the shape.** A frond's card lists every fact its handlers announce, so `fougere sync` writes
the fact's class rather than you copying its fields. Re-export it into one of your fronds, and an
arriving fact is checked against the announcer's own declaration. When the shape changes, readers
deploy before the sender: see [Changing a fact](/docs/concepts/fact#changing-a-fact).

A carrier has no address. An announcement and a listener name nobody, so a carrier can take a fact
across. A [`Pipe<T>`](/docs/behaviour/finish-a-fact) is called and answers, so it needs an address —
it crosses processes, never a carrier. `Emit<T, A>` draws the same line: it needs answers back.

[emit-multirepo](https://github.com/chok/fougere/tree/main/demos/emit-multirepo) runs two projects and
one fact, and loses the first publication on purpose to show what a carrier buys.
