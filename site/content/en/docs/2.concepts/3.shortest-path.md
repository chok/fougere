# The shortest path

Under pressure, a developer always takes the shortest path between their ticket and
"it works". That is not a moral failing, it is an economic law — and vigilance (review,
docs, good intentions) loses to it in the long run, because vigilance is paid for at
every gesture while the law never sleeps. A framework that demands virtue has already
lost. Fougere takes the law as a fact and reverses the price: **the right gesture must be
the cheapest one.**

## How a codebase drifts

Real drift does not look like bad code. It looks like a series of locally competent
patches, each placed at the point where the symptom is visible — never lifted to the
invariant that would make them unnecessary. When an invariant has no owner, every
boundary that touches it renegotiates it: six fixes in three days on the same question,
signed by three authors who never spoke, is not a talentless team — it is the signature
of a question without a seat. The patch is never the problem; the problem is that the
patch was the shortest path *available*.

## One route per destination

The shortest path is not something Fougere computes: it is a property Fougere maintains.
For every destination — reading an entity, calling an operation, judging an input —
there is **one route, and it is the shortest**. No new notion to learn in order to do it
right, no "quick" variant next to the "clean" one: the
[five client primitives](/docs/client/queries-commands) are the only path,
[`invoke`](/docs/client/invoke) is the only envelope, the
[judge](/docs/schema/entities) is the same everywhere.

Two routes to the same destination are not a documentation need: they are a framework
defect. The hack only wins when it is shorter — and it can only be shorter if the legal
route wasn't.

## The graph is an instrument

The scanner already builds the application graph to do its job: the Fronds, their
entities, their operations, the views that consume them. Exposing it turns the map into
an instrument. Three degrees can be read on it:

- **complexity** — how many edges you must know to touch a node: the degree, in the
  graph-theory sense;
- **duplication** — how many nodes state the same fact;
- **ownership** — how concentrated a question is at its owner.

Fougere pins two of them to 1 by construction: duplication, because
[declarations project](/docs/concepts/philosophy) — a fact is stated once; ownership,
because [the Frond owns](/docs/concepts/frond) — a question has a seat. What remains
is complexity, the free degree. The graph makes it visible.

What this changes: drift stops being an architect's feeling — unverifiable, exhausting,
carried by a single head — and becomes a reading. A change is judged by its delta of
degrees: "this takes duplication from 1 to 2" is a verdict anyone can render in a
minute, where the same damage, phrased in model terms, took a senior and an hour.

And the instrument has its natural home: CI. The two pinned degrees cost nothing
there — the scanner refuses at build time, a red build is enough. The free degree is
posted as a delta: every change arrives in review with its reading — "+3 edges on
`blog.Post`" — with no thresholds and no configuration. The measurement is mechanical,
the verdict stays human: the machine reads the instrument, the human judges the model.

## Boundaries teach

Forbidding is not enough: a refusal without a way out teaches circumvention. Every
refusal in the framework is therefore a declaration, like everything else:

```ts
defineRefusal({
  id: 'F-042',
  detect: (site) => isHandRolledCallToFrond(site),
  message: (site) => dedent`
    Hand-assembled call to the ${site.frond} Frond.
    The envelope carries the context (identity, locale) — a manual call loses it.

      → invoke('${site.suggestion}')
  `,
  origin: 'incident: context headers forgotten route by route',
});
```

Three beats, never more: the gesture attempted, what it breaks, the right gesture — plus
the originating incident, because a rule without its history becomes arbitrary again.
The refusal catalog derives from these declarations, the way the table derives from the
entity. It is the senior's review sentence, mechanized: available at any hour, never
tired, always at the exact moment of friction — the only moment where anyone learns.
Rust proved it for a generation: a compiler that explains shapes its users.

Framework refusals address the developer, at build time; they are distinct from
[business failures](/docs/business/errors), which travel at runtime.

## The honest limit

No framework judges meaning. The wrong concept — the false fact, declared once,
projected everywhere with perfect coherence — remains writable here as anywhere else: a
framework amplifies the model it is given. What Fougere guarantees is the *shape* of the
error: written once, at its owner, visible on the graph — the cheapest shape to find and
to fix. Lifting from symptom to invariant remains a human gesture. Everything above
exists so that this gesture is rare, short, and obvious.

Status: the graph exists inside the scanner; exposing it as an instrument and the
refusal catalog are the direction this page sets — not a shipped feature yet.
